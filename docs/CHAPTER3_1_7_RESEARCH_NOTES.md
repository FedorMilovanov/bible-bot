## Direct text and Greek boundaries

- 3:1 addresses wives toward **their own husbands**. `καὶ εἴ τινες` permits a case where **some** husbands disobey the word; it does not describe every marriage.
- 3:1–2 links the hoped-for “winning” of resistant husbands to observable `ἀναστροφή` and `ἄνευ λόγου`; this is not a doctrine of gospel-less salvation.
- `ὑποτασσόμεναι` is present passive participle, nominative plural feminine, not a finite imperative; its paraenetic force is contextual.
- `φόβῳ` is morphologically a dative of `φόβος`; morphology does not identify the referent.
- `κόσμος` is a noun; the adornment list controls its contextual sense. The normative force of `οὐχ ... ἀλλʼ` is interpretation.
- `πραέως` and `ἡσυχίου` are genitive singular neuter with `πνεύματος`; this does not lexically encode a ban on truthful speech.
- `ἀσθενεστέρῳ` is comparative dative singular neuter with `σκεύει`; morphology does not encode the dimension of weakness.
- `συγκληρονόμοις` is dative plural; `ἐγκόπτεσθαι` is present passive infinitive in `εἰς τὸ μὴ ...`.
- `κατὰ γνῶσιν` is grammatically “according to knowledge/with understanding”; the object/content of that knowledge is not expressed.

## Sarah / LXX

**Genesis 18:12 LXX:** Sarah laughs within herself and calls Abraham `κύριός μου`. 1 Pet 3:6 names Sarah and says she was `κύριον αὐτὸν καλοῦσα`. Decision: a clear named narrative reference/verbal allusion, not a formal quotation. Genesis is not a scene where Abraham commands Sarah to use the title.

**Proverbs 3:25 LXX:** `οὐ φοβηθήσῃ πτόησιν` closely parallels 1 Pet 3:6 `μὴ φοβούμεναι μηδεμίαν πτόησιν`. Decision: strong LXX background/allusion, not formal quotation; no citation formula appears and wording is adapted.

## Social history

The lane refuses a single “Greco-Roman marriage” caricature. Aristotle supplies structured-household evidence; Plutarch contains real asymmetry, including husband-led religious expectations, but also cooperation and goodwill; Musonius Rufus can emphasize companionship and mutual devotion. Treggiari demonstrates variation in Roman marriage/property/status practice. Horrell 2016 treats mixed marriage as relevant to 1 Peter while warning against overconfident historical dichotomies. Balch remains an influential social-strategy reconstruction, not direct textual fact.

Therefore the text directly envisages at least some wives whose husbands disobey the word, but the lane does **not** reconstruct one legal class, property regime, abuse profile, or mixed-marriage status for all female addressees.

## Project synthesis

The conservative/project layer reads 3:1,5 as wife-to-own-husband submission while preserving the passage's own controls: the scope is own husbands rather than all women/all men; 3:6 joins doing good with freedom from `πτόησις`; 3:7 requires husbands to live with understanding and honor; the wife is a co-heir of the grace of life; the husband's conduct is linked to hindered prayer. Davids, Schreiner, MacArthur, and Piper serve as evangelical/conservative controls; Horrell/Williams ICC, Horrell 2016, van Rensburg, Balch, ancient witnesses, and Roman social-history work prevent project conclusions from being mislabeled as neutral lexical/history facts.

## Explicit contested boundaries / HOLD

1. `φόβος` in 3:2: God-oriented fear vs husband/reverence/relational fear.
2. External adornment: strict prohibition vs rhetorical prioritization of inner character.
3. Sarah: exact theological force of Peter's reuse of Gen 18:12.
4. `πτόησις`: strong Prov 3:25 LXX connection, not formal quotation.
5. `κατὰ γνῶσιν`: content/object of the knowledge is unstated.
6. `ἀσθενεστέρῳ σκεύει`: physical, social/legal, role-related, or combined readings; no intellectual/spiritual inferiority is taught as lexical fact.
7. `ὁμοίως` in 3:7: corresponding self-limitation/service vs looser parallel transition.
8. Historical status: no universal legal/social profile for every wife in the audience.
