# 1 Peter 3:13-17 — Agent C evidence notes

Status: isolated foundation lane; **not production-reviewed and not ranking-eligible**.

## Epistemic layers

Keep separate: lexical fact, morphology, syntax, intertextual observation, theological inference, and modern application. In particular, `ἀπολογία` does **not** by itself establish classical, evidential, cumulative-case, presuppositional, or any other single modern apologetics methodology.

## Isaiah 8:12-13 and 1 Peter 3:14-15

Core comparison:

- LXX Isa 8:13: `κύριον αὐτὸν ἁγιάσατε`
- 1 Pet 3:15: `κύριον δὲ τὸν Χριστὸν ἁγιάσατε ...`

Van Rensburg and Moyise, *Scriptura* 80 (2002), argue for an explicit quotation despite no introductory formula. Their cumulative evidence includes the unusual `τὸν δὲ φόβον`, shared `φοβηθῆτε` / `ταραχθῆτε`, and the continued contrast with `ἁγιάσατε`. They also identify the substantive change directing the command to Christ as Lord.

Moyise, *Verbum et Ecclesia* 26.2 (2005), revisits the same case in a second peer-reviewed study. Because Moyise co-authored the 2002 article, this is not author-independent; Jobes and Achtemeier are therefore included as independent commentary controls.

Lane classification: **explicit quotation**, not a proposed thematic echo. The classification is evidence-based but remains analytical, not morphological.

### Christological inference

That 1 Peter puts `τὸν Χριστόν` into the Isaianic command is textual/intertextual. Judging this a significant application of Isaianic Lord-language to Christ is a theological/intertextual inference. Do not present `----ASM-` itself as proof of a doctrine.

## ἀπολογία and apologetics

- `πρὸς ἀπολογίαν` calls for readiness for a defense/answer.
- `παντὶ τῷ αἰτοῦντι` speaks of every asker in the sentence, not only a named court official.
- `λόγον περὶ τῆς ἐν ὑμῖν ἐλπίδος` keeps the requested account focused on the believers' hope.
- `μετὰ πραΰτητος καὶ φόβου` plus `συνείδησιν ἔχοντες ἀγαθήν` join answer, manner, and integrity.

Modern methods may appeal to the passage as a mandate for prepared witness; the noun itself does not choose one complete later methodology.

## 3:17 — suffering and God's will

`εἰ θέλοι τὸ θέλημα τοῦ θεοῦ` contains optative `θέλοι` in a conditional frame. The immediate contrast says suffering while doing good is preferable to suffering while doing evil if that divine-will condition obtains.

Fail-closed boundary: do not infer that every evil act by a persecutor is therefore morally approved by God, and do not turn the verse into a command to seek suffering.
